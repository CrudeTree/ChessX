# ChessX rules (owner's current draft)

Source: Djabooty, 2026-10-05. This is the rules baseline to design, playtest, and balance against.

## Game layout

- **Board:** 8x8 grid (files a to h, ranks 1 to 8). Rank 1 is Player One's back row, rank 8 is Player Two's.
- **Setup:** Kings on d1 and d8. Each side also starts with 3 Initiates from outside the deck, in front of its King (c2, d2, e2 for White; c7, d7, e7 for Black). Players draw 3 cards. Each player starts with 2 mana. White takes the first turn.
- **Resources (changed by Djabooty, 2026-10-11):** Each turn brings mana, and the amount steps up as the game goes on: +1 on your turns 1 to 5, +2 on turns 6 to 10, +3 on turns 11 to 15 and +4 from turn 16 on. Mana you don't spend carries over, with no cap. Draw 1 card per turn. Play as many cards as your mana pays for, and make one move per turn. The opening plays as before, and the big cards come within reach sooner.
- **Turn sequence (changed by Djabooty, 2026-10-10 and 2026-10-11):** Gain mana, draw a card, then play as many cards from your hand as your mana pays for: summon pieces onto your first two rows, sacrifice pieces for new ones, evolve pieces, and cast spells, in any order. You may also pitch one card, and your Dragons that know Lava Breath may spit lava. None of these ends the turn. Then move a piece. The move ends the turn, and every turn ends with a move. Only when no legal move is left, and only after you have played or pitched a card or spat lava that turn, does End turn close the turn instead, so there is still no pass. This replaces one spell, then one action (msg-034).
- **Pitching (Djabooty, 2026-10-11):** Once a turn you may pitch a card: put any card from your hand on your discard pile for +1 mana, which you can spend at once. A card you can't use yet still helps you that way.
- **Discard piles (Djabooty, 2026-10-12):** Each player has their own discard pile, face up beside their name, and either player may look through both: point at a pile to see its newest card, or click it to see them all. A spell goes on its caster's pile once it is cast. Pitched cards, sacrificed pieces, the card a piece evolved from, and sacrifices or pieces destroyed by a spell go on their owner's pile. A piece you capture goes on your pile instead, shown in your colours, so Resurrect can raise it to fight for you.
- **Casting is seen (Djabooty, 2026-10-12):** When a spell is cast, both players see it: its card covers the board for about half a second, then flies onto its caster's discard pile.
- **Under check (changed by Djabooty, 2026-10-10, 2026-10-11 and 2026-10-12):** Nothing you play may leave your own King in check. If your King is in check when your turn starts, your first play must get it out: a move (which ends the turn), or a summon onto your first two rows that blocks the check. No spell can answer a check, so spells wait until the King is safe, and so do pitching and spitting lava. Example: a Wyrmling arrives on d6 and Black's King on d8 is in check, so Black may not cast Insight or Dispel first, nor Smite the Wyrmling. Once the King is safe, the rest of the turn goes on as usual.
- **Summoning (changed by Djabooty, 2026-10-11):** Costs mana. A summoned piece goes onto any empty square of your first two rows, which are your back row and the row in front of it (ranks 1 and 2 for White, 7 and 8 for Black), but never onto lava. It used to go on the back row only. Pieces have summoning sickness: no capture on the turn they appear. They still attack squares, so they give check at once. A summoned piece also cannot move until your next turn (changed by Djabooty, 2026-10-10).
- **Sacrificing (changed by Djabooty, 2026-10-11):** Costs mana. Place a card face down on one of your pieces to sacrifice that piece for it. The face-down card is the sacrifice: it cannot move or capture and can be destroyed by an enemy. Its timer counts down on each of your turns. When it hits 0, the new piece arrives on that square. This was called sealing; players asked what a seal was, so it is now a sacrifice.
- **Evolving (changed by Djabooty, 2026-10-11):** Costs mana. Level 2 and level 3 cards of an evolution line each name the card they evolve from. Play one onto your piece of that card and the piece changes into it at once, on the same square. A level 2 card may also go onto an Initiate, which takes any path (see Evolution lines). Only a piece that was already on the board when your turn began can evolve. The evolved piece waits like a summoned one: no move or capture until your next turn, though it gives check at once. Evolution cards are only ever played this way, never summoned or sacrificed for.
- **Capture bounty (Djabooty, 2026-10-11):** Capturing an enemy piece pays you half its card's mana cost, rounded up: 1 for a Recruit or Squire, 2 for a Warrior, 5 for a Duskfang. Initiates cost 0 and pay nothing, and destroying a sacrifice pays nothing either.
- **Game log (Djabooty, 2026-10-11):** The game keeps its whole log. A player who leaves a game and opens it again sees everything that happened in it, not just the last turn.
- **Winning:** Checkmate the enemy King. Normal chess check rules apply.

## Evolution lines (Djabooty, 2026-10-11)

The rook, bishop, knight and queen are the top of a line, never a card you summon. Level 1 is summoned or sacrificed for like any piece; levels 2 and 3 only evolve from the level below. The weaker the first level, the stronger the last. Costs are placeholders to playtest.

**Costs (changed by Djabooty, 2026-10-11):** Pieces were too easy to get, so the costs went up and a Paladin now costs at least 4. Every level costs more than the one below it. A first level that leaps off the back row (Colt, Wyrmling) costs 2; one that steps (Recruit, Apprentice) costs 1.

- **Warrior (the rook):** Recruit (1 mana, steps 1 square straight), then Warrior (4, slides up to 3 squares straight), then Warlord (6, slides straight any distance).
- **Mage (the bishop):** Apprentice (1, steps 1 square diagonally), then Mage (3, slides up to 3 squares diagonally), then Archmage (4, slides diagonally any distance).
- **Paladin (the knight):** Colt (2, knight leaps that go forward), then Paladin (4, leaps like a knight), then Dawn Paladin (7, a knight leap or 1 square straight) or Eclipse Knight (7, a knight leap or 1 square diagonally).
- **Dragon (the queen):** Wyrmling (2, leaps exactly 2 squares straight), then Mystic Dragon (6, slides any direction up to 2 squares), then Ancient Mystic Dragon (10, slides any direction any distance). The Mystic Dragon reached 3 squares, which made it a queen too early (changed by Djabooty, 2026-10-11).
- **How soon (changed by Djabooty, 2026-10-11):** A player who saves for nothing else, without pitching or capturing, can have a Paladin on turn 4 at the earliest, an Archmage on turn 6, a Warlord on turn 7, a Dawn Paladin or Eclipse Knight on turn 8 and an Ancient Mystic Dragon on turn 11. With +1 mana every turn the last three came on turns 9, 11 and 16.
- **No line (changed by Djabooty, 2026-10-11):** The Squire is a pawn and does not evolve. Duskfang has no line, so it costs 9 to make up for it. It cost 15, which made it a card nobody could play.
- **Any path (Djabooty, 2026-10-11):** The Initiate is the other pawn. It has no line of its own but counts as the first level of every line, so any level 2 card can be played onto it: a Warrior, Mage, Paladin or Mystic Dragon. That costs the card's mana plus the mana of the first level it skips, so an Initiate becomes a Mage for 4 (3, plus 1 for the Apprentice) or a Mystic Dragon for 8 (6, plus 2 for the Wyrmling). From there it climbs the line as usual.
- **Where the old cards went:** Page became the Recruit, Hopper the Paladin, Cathedral Runner the Archmage and Tower the Warlord. Dawnfang moved exactly like Eclipse Knight and was folded into it. Games already in progress keep the cards they started with.
- **Starter deck (changed by Djabooty, 2026-10-11 and 2026-10-12):** 30 cards: 2 Initiates and 1 Squire; for each line two level 1 cards and one of every higher level (both Paladin final forms); 1 Duskfang; 2 Insight, and one each of Dispel, Lava Breath, Mana Surge, Resurrect, Shield, Freeze and Smite.
- **Games in progress (Djabooty, 2026-10-11):** A game that began before these rulings keeps its cards as they were: their costs and moves, Initiates that do not take any path, and no Lava Breath. The rules of the turn apply to it at once: the step-up income, pitching, capture bounties and summoning onto the first two rows. Its log is kept in full from then on.
- **Games in progress (Djabooty, 2026-10-12):** A game that began before the five new spells keeps its deck without them. The discard pile rules apply to it at once.

## Card types (Djabooty, 2026-10-11)

Every card names its type. A line's cards are of the line's type: Warrior (Recruit, Warrior, Warlord), Mage (Apprentice, Mage, Archmage), Paladin (Colt, Paladin, Dawn Paladin, Eclipse Knight) and Dragon (Wyrmling, Mystic Dragon, Ancient Mystic Dragon). The Squire is a Warrior, the Duskfang a Beast and the Initiate a Zealot. A spell made for a type can only be played onto your own pieces of that type. Lava Breath is the first, for Dragons; spells for Warriors, Mages, Paladins and the other types can follow.

## Spells

- **Insight (2 mana, changed by Djabooty, 2026-10-11):** Draw 1 card and gain 1 mana. It used to draw 2 cards; now it pays half its cost back, so digging for a card costs less.
- **Dispel (3 mana):** Destroy a sacrifice before its piece arrives. Its card goes to its owner's discard pile.
- **Lava Breath (2 mana, a Dragon spell; Djabooty, 2026-10-11):** Play it onto one of your Dragons. That piece learns Lava Breath and keeps it for the rest of the game, through every evolution.
    - **Spitting lava:** Once a turn, a Dragon that knows Lava Breath may spend 1 mana to spit lava onto an empty square it could move to. This does not end the turn. A Dragon that was summoned or evolved this turn waits until your next turn, and nothing spits lava while your King is in check.
    - **Lava:** No piece, yours or the enemy's, can move onto a lava square or be summoned onto it until the other player has taken 2 more turns. It cools when your turn after that begins. Lava holds a square, not a line: pieces pass over it, and attacks and checks go across it, so it never answers a check. It can shut a King's escape square or a square the enemy wants to summon onto.
- **Mana Surge (1 mana; Djabooty, 2026-10-12):** Gain 3 mana, which leaves you 2 ahead.
- **Resurrect (4 mana; Djabooty, 2026-10-12):** Summon a piece card that costs 4 or less from your discard pile onto a free square of your first two rows, never lava. It can be one of your own pieces that left play or an enemy piece you captured; either way it rises as your piece, in your colours. It waits like a summoned piece: no move or capture until your next turn, though it gives check at once. When more than one card could rise, you pick which.
- **Shield (2 mana; Djabooty, 2026-10-12):** Play it onto one of your pieces (not the King). Until your next turn nothing can capture that piece and no enemy spell can target it. It can still move and capture.
- **Freeze (2 mana; Djabooty, 2026-10-12):** Play it onto an enemy piece that is not the King and not shielded. On its owner's next turn it cannot move or capture. It still attacks squares and gives check.
- **Smite (3 mana; Djabooty, 2026-10-12):** Destroy an enemy piece that is not the King and not shielded, and whose card costs 2 or less. It is not a capture: it pays no bounty, and the card goes on its owner's discard pile. Smite cannot remove a piece if that would leave your own King in check.

## Card faces (Djabooty, 2026-10-11)

Every card has two faces: a light face for the player with White and a dark face for the player with Black. Owning a card gives you both. The light face looks holy, majestic and noble (think Gandalf the White); the dark face looks mighty, powerful and dark (think Sauron). Every current card has both faces, and its pictures show how strong it is: first levels such as the Recruit, Colt and Wyrmling look humble, final forms look mighty, and the Ancient Mystic Dragon outshines them all (changed by Djabooty, 2026-10-11). The Initiate, Squire and Apprentice are grown men, plainly equipped but threatening, never children: the Initiate is a hooded zealot (White) or cultist (Black) with a staff, the Squire a scarred man-at-arms with sword and shield, and the Apprentice a young wizard with only a small spark of lightning (changed by Djabooty, 2026-10-11). A new card that has only one picture shows it to both sides until it gets its second face.

**Type colours (Djabooty, 2026-10-12):** Pieces looked too much alike from across the board; the dark faces were all red and black. Every type now has its own colour, on both faces: Warrior red, Mage violet, Paladin gold, Dragon green, Beast blue and Zealot ash (plain white on the light face). The dark faces glow in it; the light faces take a soft wash of it in their sky and light. On the board, a piece's ring is in its type's colour, and its badge shows the chess piece its line moves like with its level: ♜ for Warriors, ♝ for Mages, ♞ for Paladins and ♛ for Dragons, while the Squire, Initiate and Duskfang show their own sign (♟, ✚, ☾). A card prints its type in the same colour. The King has no type and keeps its side's colours. New pictures for a type keep to its colour.

## What was removed

- **Attack and defense stats.** Combat is pure chess capture: move into a square to take it. No ATK/DEF comparison and no hit points.
- **Piece-generated mana (changed by Djabooty, 2026-10-11).** Pieces still make no mana. Mana comes only from each turn's income, one pitch a turn and capture bounties.
- **Traditional starting pieces.** No starting pawns, rooks, knights, bishops, or queens. Each player starts with a King and 3 Initiates and summons the rest of the army.
- **Summoning a finished chess piece (changed by Djabooty, 2026-10-11).** The Tower, Cathedral Runner and Hopper could be summoned outright. The rook, bishop, knight and queen now sit at the top of evolution lines, and only their first level is summoned.
- **Mana cap.** Mana can be banked without limit.
- **Tribute cost.** Summoning costs mana. Tributing a piece is no longer a cost. It is now the placement choice for the sacrifice mechanic.
- **Pieces sharing a square.** One piece per square, always.

## Rulings (decided by Djabooty, 2026-10-05)

- **Arriving:** A sacrifice turns into the card that was placed face down. The choice is made when the sacrifice is made, not when the piece arrives. That makes a sacrifice a readable, predictable threat the opponent can plan around for its whole timer.
- **Sacrificed piece (changed by Djabooty, 2026-10-12):** The piece underneath is consumed. It is removed from play and its card goes on your discard pile, where only Resurrect can bring it back. This is the cost of sacrificing: you gain position (summoning deep) and tempo (the new piece appears on a timer, in place), but lose the body you sacrificed.
- **Destroying a sacrifice (changed by Djabooty, 2026-10-12):** Capturing the sacrifice's square destroys it. An enemy piece moves onto that square, and the face-down card goes on its owner's discard pile, unlike a captured piece, which goes on its captor's. A sacrifice is a piece and a sitting duck, and that is its weakness. A spell may destroy sacrifices only if that spell says so. It is not a general rule.
- **Summoning sickness:** Summoned, evolved and arrived pieces have it. They wait until their owner's next turn to capture. A summoned or evolved piece also waits until then to move, while a piece that arrived from a sacrifice may move at once (changed by Djabooty, 2026-10-10; evolved pieces added 2026-10-11). The 3 starting Initiates do not have it. They count as already on the board before the game began and can act normally on turn 1.
- **Check is a board fact (changed by Djabooty, 2026-10-08):** A sick piece cannot capture, but it still attacks the squares it reaches. It gives check the moment it appears, and the enemy King cannot step into its reach. Example: a Wyrmling arrives on d6, Black must answer check on d8 at once, and no King step into the Wyrmling's reach is legal. The same holds for summoned and evolved pieces. This replaces the 10-05 ruling that sick pieces cannot give check.
- **Starting pieces (changed by Djabooty, 2026-10-08):** Each side gets 3 Initiates from outside the deck, placed on c2, d2 and e2 (White) or c7, d7 and e7 (Black) before turn 1, with no summoning sickness. They shield the King from instant check and leave the back row free for summoning. Players keep their whole opening hand. The starter deck keeps 2 Initiates as mid-game free bodies. This replaces the opening where players deployed 3 zero-cost pieces from their hand to the back row.
- **Opening hand (changed by Djabooty, 2026-10-10):** Players draw 3 cards before the game instead of 7. The draw of 1 card at the start of every turn is unchanged, so White holds 4 cards on its first turn. Hands were piling up: a player who kept every card held 17 on their 10th turn, and now holds 13.
- **Costs (changed by Djabooty, 2026-10-11):** Any piece card in hand can be summoned or sacrificed for, except an evolution card, which can only evolve a piece. The cost is the mana value printed on the card. Mana grows by +1 a turn at first and steps up to +4 a turn later (changed by Djabooty, 2026-10-11), so that growth is the pacing of the game. A 3-cost piece is a mid-game investment and a 5-cost piece is a game-defining play that takes several turns of saving. A line spreads its top piece over several turns: a Wyrmling, a Mystic Dragon and an Ancient Mystic Dragon cost 18 mana in all.
- **No legal action (changed by Djabooty, 2026-10-10):** Stalemate. A player cannot pass. If a player has no legal move and no card they can play when their turn starts, and their King is not in check, the game is a draw by stalemate. If the King is in check and nothing resolves it, that is checkmate. A player who can still play a card is not stalemated: they play it, and End turn closes the turn if no move is left.
- **Where a sacrifice can go:** Only on one of your own non-King pieces. A King can never be sacrificed, because it is your win condition and cannot be used as a resource.
- **Timer length:** The timer is printed on the card. It is a core balancing lever. A cheap, low-impact piece might have a timer of 1, while a game-changing piece might have a timer of 3, giving the opponent more time to respond. Evolution cards have no timer (Djabooty, 2026-10-11).
- **Blocking:** A sacrifice blocks movement and lines of attack exactly like any other piece. It is an occupying piece on the board, which is its main defensive use and why sacrificing a front-line piece is risky but rewarding.
- **Stalemate test (changed by Djabooty, 2026-10-10):** A sacrifice is a piece for blocking squares and for being captured, but it has no legal move and does not count as a piece that can act. If a player's only pieces are the King and one or more sacrifices, the King has no legal move, and they have no card they can play, that player is in stalemate.
- **Arrival timing:** A piece cannot arrive onto a square an enemy has moved onto. The sacrifice must survive until the start of its owner's turn, when the timer resolves. If an enemy captures the sacrifice's square first, the sacrifice and its card are lost. The piece only arrives if the square is still under its owner's control at that moment.
- **Timer timing:** A sacrifice's timer counts down at the start of each of its owner's turns, and the piece arrives when it reaches 0. A timer of 1 arrives at the start of the owner's next turn, so the opponent gets exactly one move in between.
- **Evolving in place (Djabooty, 2026-10-11):** Evolving is instant. The old piece leaves play, its card going on your discard pile (Djabooty, 2026-10-12), and the new one stands on its square, with no timer and nothing for the enemy to destroy. A piece climbs at most one level per turn, because a piece that evolved this turn was not on the board when the turn began. Evolving never answers a check: the square stays occupied, so the King stays attacked.
- **Branching line (Djabooty, 2026-10-11):** A Paladin can become either a Dawn Paladin or an Eclipse Knight. The card you play decides.
- **Empty deck (changed by congress, msg-033, 2026-10-10):** When a deck runs out, the draw is skipped. There is no deck-out loss; checkmate stays the only win.
- **No promotion (changed by congress, msg-033, 2026-10-10; example changed by Djabooty, 2026-10-11):** A piece that reaches the far rank stays as it is. It can still be sacrificed like any other piece. Sacrifice is the crown: a Squire that reaches e8 can be sacrificed for a Wyrmling (cost 2, timer 1), which can then evolve into a Mystic Dragon and an Ancient Mystic Dragon, the queen.

## Open questions

- **Spells for the other types (Djabooty's idea, 2026-10-11):** Lava Breath gives Dragons a power of their own. Warriors, Mages, Paladins, the Beast and the Zealot could each get a spell like it.
- **A fifth line (Djabooty's idea, 2026-10-11):** a first level that moves like a King, whose final form is a queen that reaches only 5 squares in every direction.
